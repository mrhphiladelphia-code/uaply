AI and matching

Last updated October 12, 2026.

Draft, not reviewed by a lawyer. Laws on automated tools in hiring are changing quickly. Have an employment attorney check the notices below, and keep this page true if the product changes.

The short version

  • The match percentage on each card is a fixed formula, not artificial intelligence or machine learning.
  • It only helps order jobs for you. It never rejects you, hides you from an employer, or decides anything about hiring.
  • Aply does not use generative AI on your profile or applications today. If we add it, we will tell you here and in the app first, and ask before using your information with it.
  • Employers make every hiring decision.

How the match score works

For each listing, Aply compares what the listing asks for with what you entered, and adds points:

PartPointsHow it is judged
Skills40The share of the listing's skills that are in your profile
Experience20Your years of experience against the years the listing asks for
Pay15Whether the listed pay reaches your minimum (partial credit if pay is not listed)
Work style10Remote, hybrid or on-site against what you accept
Location10Your city against the listing's location, or willingness to relocate
Education5Your highest education against what the listing asks for

In some versions of Aply the score is also capped at 45% when pay, work style or experience is a clear miss, and the card says so. Even then the listing is never hidden from you.

Each card shows the points for every part, so you can see exactly why a score is what it is. You can ignore the score and swipe on any listing. You can also set your own minimum match, or leave it at zero to see everything.

What the score does not use

Aply does not ask for or use your age, race, ethnicity, sex, gender, religion, national origin, disability, veteran status or similar characteristics. Your optional work authorization answers are not part of the score. We know that inputs like location and education can sometimes track protected characteristics indirectly, which is why the score never removes a listing on its own and why we show the full breakdown.

Notice for job candidates

When you apply through Aply, the employer receives the details you chose to give. Aply does not screen, rank or reject applicants on an employer's behalf. If an employer uses its own automated tool to evaluate you, that employer, not Aply, is responsible for telling you and for following the law that applies to it. Examples of those laws, current as of this page's date:

  • New York City (Local Law 144): employers using automated employment decision tools must have a recent independent bias audit, post a summary, and notify candidates.
  • Illinois (amended Human Rights Act, in effect January 1, 2026): employers must not use AI that discriminates and must notify people when AI is used in employment decisions.
  • California: Civil Rights Council rules (in effect October 1, 2025) cover automated decision systems in employment, and the California Consumer Privacy Act's rules on automated decision-making technology have compliance dates beginning in 2026 and 2027.
  • Colorado: a replacement automated decision-making law takes effect January 1, 2027.

Rules differ by place and change often. Ask an employer directly if you want to know whether it uses automated tools on applications.

For employers

If you receive applications through Aply, you stay responsible for your own hiring decisions and for any legal notices, audits and accommodations that apply to the tools you use. Aply's match score is a sorting aid for candidates, not an assessment of them, and you should not treat it as one.

Changes

If Aply begins using machine learning, generative AI or any automated step that affects what an employer sees about you, we will update this page and tell you in the app before it applies to you.
